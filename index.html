Australia’s Biodiversity Collapse

Across forests, reefs and wetlands, environmental pressures are accelerating faster than ecosystems can recover.

A continent under ecological pressure

Australia is experiencing one of the fastest biodiversity declines on Earth. Across forests, reefs and wetlands, entire ecosystems are beginning to unravel under mounting ecological pressure.

Bushfires, climate disruption, habitat destruction and invasive species are reshaping the continent at an accelerating pace. This visualisation explores where ecological collapse is unfolding, what is driving it, and why current conservation efforts may no longer be enough.

Extinction is rarely caused by a single threat

Australia’s biodiversity crisis is driven by overlapping environmental pressures. Habitat destruction, invasive predators, climate change and extreme bushfire events reinforce one another, accelerating ecological collapse across already fragile ecosystems.

KEY INSIGHT

Environmental threats reinforce one another

Habitat loss and climate change continue to place the greatest pressure on threatened mammals and bird populations.

Ecological vulnerability increases under compounding stress

Species exposed to multiple environmental pressures are significantly more vulnerable to long-term population decline.

Environmental pressures are accelerating over time

The biodiversity crisis is not static. Over the past two decades, climate stress, bushfire severity and habitat destruction have intensified simultaneously, creating compounding ecological pressures across Australia.

Regional pressure is unevenly distributed

Extinction pressure varies sharply across Australia. Amphibians and mammals now face especially severe threats in climate-sensitive and densely populated regions.

Conservation funding remains unevenly distributed

Conservation funding remains unevenly distributed. While bushfire recovery and habitat restoration receive growing investment, long-term biodiversity monitoring and species recovery programs continue to face critical funding gaps.

Wildlife populations have been declining for decades

Ecological collapse rarely occurs suddenly. Across multiple Australian ecosystems, wildlife populations have declined steadily for decades as environmental pressures continue to intensify.
